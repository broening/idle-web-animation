Parts are read in name order, front first — name them 1-head, 2-arm and so on. Order is not final: drag the rows below and save. Uploading runs tools/import-layers.py, which keeps a rig you have already corrected and only replaces the pixels.

Layers

Pick a layer, then drag its dot on the stage to move the pivot. The number lands straight in figure.json.

Drag a row by its grip to change draw order — the list runs front to back. The dot switches a layer off on the stage only; the contact sheet still shows what ships.

    Motion

    Check

    A single screenshot cannot prove movement. The contact sheet renders 24 frames of the 8 second window into one image.

    A blink holds the eye closed for 86 ms and the sheet samples every 333 ms, so it catches about one in four - and on a figure whose blinks never land on that grid, none at all. Events scans 30 seconds at 60 frames and lists what actually fires.

    Cut test (IoU)
    
      
    Load a machine-cut layer and compare it against the
      hand-made one. IoU is the overlap of the two shapes: 1.00 is identical,
      below about 0.85 the part will not line up.
    
      
    The layerize models name their own output, so a filename
      rarely matches. Auto-match compares each file against every truth layer
      and keeps the best, and flags a win that was too close to call.
