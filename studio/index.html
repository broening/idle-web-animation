      how marking works

      One rough blob per part, each in its own colour. Paint inside the part — the boundary is worked out from the picture, not from your blob.

      The finer the part, the closer to its edge you have to paint. A thin sabre marked with a dot loses half of itself to the jaw beside it; marked along its length it does not.

      Alt or the right button erases.

      reach is how far a mark may spread beyond what you painted, and it is the setting that decides everything. At 24 px the cut follows your brush. Wind it up and the marks divide the whole figure between them, which puts the seams halfway between your blobs rather than on the drawing.

      Whatever no mark reached becomes one layer at the back. Switch that off to keep only the parts you marked — for taking two limbs out of this picture and the rest out of another.

      Names decide the rig. kopf or head gives a neck pivot and a gaze, arm a shoulder and a sway. The field offers every word that works.

      Cutting a flat picture can only recover what is visible. What lies behind a part is not in the file, so a part that moves far shows a hole. Measured on the priest: a pupil with 6 visible pixels cannot be cut out at all — eyes want their own drawn images.

      how the list works

      The list runs front to back, the way a layer palette does. Drag a row by its grip to change draw order.

      The dot switches a layer off on the stage only — the contact sheet still shows what ships.

      Pick a layer, then drag its dot on the stage to move the pivot. To move the picture itself, hold Alt and drag, or use the arrow keys — 1 px a step, Shift for 10. That writes offset and leaves the pivot where it is.

      The wheel zooms towards the pointer. A second wheel or a tilt wheel slides sideways, and so does Shift with the main one. Dragging anywhere that is not a dot pushes the figure about, and so does the middle button. Fit, or a double-click, puts it back. None of that is saved — it is a magnifying glass, not a size.

      Parts upload in name order, front first — name them 1-head, 2-arm. Uploading keeps a rig you have already corrected and only replaces the pixels.

      Mood
      how moods work

      A mood is a second look for the same rig: a lower head, a slower breath, another mouth. Editing one here writes only the differences into figure.states — the rig itself (pivot, parent, depth, role, blend, lag, opacity, alt, which motions a layer has) stays locked while a mood is selected, because every mood shares one rig.

      With a mood picked, the stage, the contact sheet and Events show exactly that mood, with no blend — what you see here is where it ends up, not a mix on the way there.

      Motion
      why once each

      Each type once per layer. Two blinks read the same clock and fire as one; two drifts make the layer jump between rises.

      Add part

      Take a layer out of another figure and put it on this one. Any canvas size: it is scaled and baked into this figure's canvas, so what ships is an ordinary full-canvas layer and the player needs to know nothing about it.

      Drag it on the stage to move it.

      why this exists

      Cut one picture for the parts it does well, cut a second for the parts it does better, and put them on one figure. The two pictures do not have to be the same size.

      The part arrives with a sway and no parent, because nothing about a picture says where it hangs. Set that in the Layer card — one dropdown.

      Upload parts… at the top is the other way in, and the faster one when the images already fit: it takes several at once, but every one has to be the figure's exact canvas size. This card takes one image of any size and scales it.

          why two views
          
      A single screenshot cannot prove movement. The
          contact sheet renders 24 frames of the 8 second window into one
          image.
      
          
      A blink holds the eye closed for 86 ms and the sheet
          samples every 333 ms, so it catches about one in four — and on
          a figure whose blinks never land on that grid, none at all. Events
          scans 30 seconds at 60 frames and lists what actually fires.
      
        
      
      
      

      
      
        Export
        
      
          
          
        
      
        
      
        
      Master layers stay at full resolution. Pick the size
        the target wants and take a zip.
      
        
      Every layer is cut to the part it paints, and
        figure.json gets a crop saying where that
        part sits. The rig does not change; the decoded memory drops by an
        order of magnitude. The figures in this folder stay uncut.
      

        
      OBS package. A second zip: the figure as
        an OBS Browser source, no Python or server needed once it is handed
        over — idle.js, idle.css and the scene script all ride along
        inside one obs.html. The size above decides how big its
        layers are too. The figure's own background never comes along; the
        page stays transparent.
      
        
      A logo always ships as its own OBS source,
        logo.html, which you move and scale in OBS. Tick
        logo inside figure to also bake it into obs.html
        at logo X, Y and width.

          what the number means
          
      IoU is the overlap of the two shapes: 1.00 is
          identical, below about 0.85 the part will not line up.
      
          
      The layerize models name their own output, so a

          truth layer, keeps the best, and flags a win that was too close to
